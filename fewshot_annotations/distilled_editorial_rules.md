# Distilled Editorial Rules For Weather Annotations

These rules are inferred from the filled feedback sheet. They are meant to capture editorial judgment, not to copy the exact wording of the example headlines.

## Strong patterns in the feedback

- Lead with the **main event day or short event cluster** when a large share of rain comes from one burst.
- When rain clearly changes temperatures, mention the **cooling effect** explicitly rather than just rainfall totals.
- Prefer **temperature anomalies** over rainfall aggregates when the temperature departure is the more surprising part of the story.
- When a dry spell is the story, pair **run length + aggregate shortfall**. Example form: "`Dry spell from ... Just X mm rain vs Y expected`".
- When records occur on most days in the window, the **record count takes precedence** over weaker secondary signals.
- Warm or cool **nights** are distinct stories; do not flatten them into generic temperature commentary.
- If rain is widespread across the full window, say that directly: **"no dry days" / "rained every day"** is stronger than generic "wet period".
- If the window is wet overall but mostly because of 1-2 days, do **not** call it sustained.
- Use a **seasonal frame** when that sharpens the story: "winter", "March", "October", "November", "hot and dry November", etc.

## Negative guidance

- Do not overstate antecedent streaks if they are not visually or substantively the main story.
- Do not default to the rainfall story when rain mainly matters because it triggered a temperature break.
- Do not call a month-long wet patch "accumulation" if the real signal is a short deluge inside that month.
- Do not combine too many signals. Pick one dominant frame unless a second signal clearly strengthens it.

## Preferred signal hierarchy

This is approximate, not absolute:

1. Record clusters across most of the window
2. Very large temperature departures, especially sustained heat or unusual coolth
3. Heavy rain bursts with clear temperature impact
4. Persistent all-window wetness or dryness
5. Aggregate monthly excess/deficit when there is no sharper event-driven story

## Useful headline templates

These are pattern templates, not text to copy verbatim:

- `Heavy rain on [dates] led to sharp drop in temperatures`
- `Sustained heat wave for past [window] with both highs and lows [X] above normal`
- `Dry spell from [date] to [date]. Just [X]mm rain vs [Y]mm expected`
- `Record high temperatures for the last [window]. Hot and dry [month]!`
- `Cold nights and no rain since [date]. Lows [X] below expected`
- `Wet [month], primarily driven by deluge on [dates]`
