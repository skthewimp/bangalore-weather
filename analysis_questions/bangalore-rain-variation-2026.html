How uneven was Bangalore’s rain this monsoon?

Codex

2026-10-01

What are we trying to see?

The first instinct is to draw one daily-rain line for every locality. That is useful as a raw view, but it becomes spaghetti quickly because most days are zero and the interesting comparison is really two things:

  1. When did rain arrive, and where? A date-by-locality heatmap is better for this than 28 overlapping lines.
  2. How much accumulated over the season? A ranked horizontal chart makes the seasonal differences directly comparable without the identification problem of 28 overlapping cumulative lines.

The unit is a locality-day. Rain is the sum of WeatherUnion’s derived hourly rain_mm, but only on days with 24 valid hourly rain values. This is station rainfall, not an interpolated ward or city-wide surface.

First, check coverage

archive <- open_dataset(file.path(root_dir, "data", "weatherunion_bengaluru_hourly.parquet"))

hourly <- archive %>%
  filter(
    hour_ist >= as.POSIXct("2026-05-01 00:00:00", tz = "Asia/Kolkata"),
    hour_ist < as.POSIXct("2026-10-01 00:00:00", tz = "Asia/Kolkata")
  ) %>%
  select(locality_id, current_locality_name, hour_ist, rain_mm) %>%
  collect() %>%
  mutate(date = as.Date(hour_ist, tz = "Asia/Kolkata"))

stopifnot(!anyDuplicated(hourly[c("locality_id", "hour_ist")]))
stopifnot(all(hourly$rain_mm >= 0 | is.na(hourly$rain_mm)))

daily_raw <- hourly %>%
  group_by(locality_id, current_locality_name, date) %>%
  summarise(
    observed_hours = n(),
    valid_rain_hours = sum(!is.na(rain_mm)),
    rain_mm = sum(rain_mm, na.rm = TRUE),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) %>%
  mutate(
    # Missing hourly rain is not silently treated as a complete day's total.
    rain_mm = if_else(valid_rain_hours == 24, rain_mm, NA_real_)
  )

coverage <- daily_raw %>%
  summarise(
    days_in_window = n(),
    complete_days = sum(!is.na(rain_mm)),
    first_day = min(date),
    last_day = max(date),
    .by = c(locality_id, current_locality_name)
  ) %>%
  arrange(desc(complete_days), current_locality_name)

There are 31 localities with at least some data in the requested window. To avoid letting short or late-starting sensors distort the comparison, I keep the 28 localities with at least 120 complete rain-days. Three localities are excluded from the main comparison because their coverage is too short or incomplete for a May–September seasonal total. The excluded localities are retained in the coverage table above rather than silently disappearing.

The daily pattern: rain was episodic and spatially patchy

selected <- coverage %>%
  filter(complete_days >= 120) %>%
  pull(current_locality_name)

row_totals <- daily_raw %>%
  filter(current_locality_name %in% selected) %>%
  group_by(current_locality_name) %>%
  summarise(seasonal_rain_mm = sum(rain_mm, na.rm = TRUE), .groups = "drop")

plot_daily <- daily_raw %>%
  filter(current_locality_name %in% selected) %>%
  left_join(row_totals, by = "current_locality_name") %>%
  mutate(
    # Alphabetical order makes the heatmap usable as a locality lookup table.
    current_locality_name = factor(
      current_locality_name,
      levels = rev(sort(unique(as.character(current_locality_name))))
    ),
    rain_for_tile = replace_na(rain_mm, 0),
    coverage = if_else(is.na(rain_mm), "Incomplete", "Complete")
  )

heat_path <- file.path(chart_dir, "bangalore-rain-variation-2026-daily-heatmap.png")
heat <- ggplot() +

The heatmap is the main chart because the daily values are mostly zero, with rainfall concentrated in a relatively small number of event days. It lets us see whether a wet day was broad-based or confined to a few parts of Bangalore without forcing the reader to trace dozens of overlapping lines.

The row-end totals make the heatmap self-contained: locality order supports lookup, while the totals provide the seasonal comparison without a second chart.

What stands out?

In this snapshot, MG Road is the wettest of the selected stations at about 702 mm, while Jalahalli is the driest at about 230 mm. That is a spread of roughly 472 mm, or just over three times from the driest selected station to the wettest. This is enough to make “Bangalore rainfall” a misleading shorthand for this period: the archive is showing station-level spatial variation, not one uniform city experience.

Verdict

Insightful: yes

The heatmap should be the single publishable chart: it carries event timing, spatial patchiness, and each station’s May–September total in one view. This remains an observational WeatherUnion comparison, not an estimate of rainfall across every neighbourhood or a causal explanation of why one area was wetter.
